🏠 Room temperature tips — for plants in an office, dining room or living area

No garage or basement space? Plenty of plants overwinter fine in a regular room (~68–72°F). They stay active, so they need more water and light than dormant plants — and heated rooms are dry.

  • Humidity tray: set pots on a tray of pebbles with water just below the pot bottoms, or group plants together. A small humidifier helps too.
  • Mind the heat: keep plants away from heat vents, radiators and space heaters — and off cold, drafty windowsills at night.
  • Grow light on a timer: 12–16 hours a day, a few inches to a foot above the leaves. A plug-in timer makes it automatic.
  • Water by feel: check the soil twice a week; water when the top inch is dry, then let it drain. Never leave pots sitting in water.
  • Pest patrol: warm, dry air brings spider mites and aphids. Check leaf undersides weekly and rinse foliage in the sink or shower if needed.
  • Airflow: a gentle fan reduces mildew and makes stems sturdier.
  • Go easy on fertilizer until late winter, when days get longer.

Have a cool spot instead (garage, basement, someplace cool)? Most cut-back peppers, eggplants, figs and lemongrass rest dormant there with very little water and light — switch the plant to ❄️ Cool spot on its card. Tomato and basil cuttings should stay warm.

🌿 All care tips and watering intervals are general guidance only. Every home is different — always check the soil with your finger before watering and adjust intervals to suit your plants.
